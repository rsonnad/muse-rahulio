Read this first — this page in plain English.
Bedrooms: Portuguese listings use a code — T0 means a studio, T1 a 1-bedroom, T2 a 2-bedroom, T3 a 3-bedroom, T4 a 4-bedroom home. The number counts bedrooms only, never the living room. Everywhere else on this page that code is written out in English (“2-bedroom”, “3-bedroom”), so you do not need the code to read the tables.
Layout: one table for rentals, one table for purchases — the Photo column sits inside each table, next to its listing. R = rental, P = purchase. Scroll a table sideways for all its columns; the dark-green column headers stay pinned at the top while you scroll down.
Sizes & money: areas are in square meters (m²) — 100 m² is about 1,076 sq ft. Prices are in US dollars first, euros in brackets. “Condo fees” are the building’s monthly charges. Portuguese tax words appear in brackets after their English meaning, because those are the words you will see on local paperwork.
How to read this page — honesty notes. Every price, area and amenity below came from a live listing page checked 1–2 Nov 2026. Where a listing did not state something, the tables say “not stated” — nothing is filled in by guessing. Photos labelled “Listing photo” are that unit's own photo from its listing. Photos labelled “Development photo” show the building/development (e.g. Prata Riverside Village), not that specific unit. Listings flagged ⚠️ state a figure two ways inside their own listing — the weekly update re-checks them (most first-crawl flags were resolved on 2 Nov 2026, see the update log). Condo fees were not published on any sale listing found, so budget them separately (see Taxes & Fees).

Taxes & fees — the full cost picture

Verified 2 Nov 2026 for 2025–2026 rules from Portal das Finanças–based guides, PwC Tax Summaries, CMS and specialist law-firm guides. Rates are set annually — confirm any deal in the AT (Autoridade Tributária) simulator / with a Portuguese lawyer before a deed. Re-checked 2 Nov 2026: no IMT / IMI / stamp-duty / rental-cap rule changed since 1 Nov 2026. Watch items (proposals only, not law): the OE2027 State Budget (final vote 24 Nov 2026) would lift all IMT thresholds by 2.3% for 2027 with rates unchanged, and a government lease-reform proposal would raise the advance cap to 3 months and remove the fixed deposit cap — until either is published, the rules below stand. Full sources at the foot of this section.

⚠️ Critical 2026 change: from 25 May 2026, a buyer who is not a Portuguese tax resident pays a flat 7.5% IMT on urban residential property — no brackets, no exemptions (Decreto-Lei 97/2026). You can claim a refund of the difference vs resident rates if you become PT tax resident within 2 years, or let the home at a moderate rent ≤ €2,300/mo (lease signed within 6 months, kept ≥ 36 months in the first 5 years). This single rule swings a €620,000 purchase by about €10,700 — see the worked examples.

Renting in — what a tenant should (and should not) pay

ItemRule (in force since 1 Jan 2023)
Deposit (caução)Capped at 2 months' rent
Advance rentCapped at 2 months, and only if agreed in writing. With the first month, the lawful maximum at signing is 5 months' rent in total — demands for 6–12 months upfront exceed the caps (several listings above ask for guarantor + 2 + 2, which is within the caps)
Guarantor (fiador)Not required by law, but commonly requested in Lisbon, especially without PT employment / rental history. Negotiated alternatives: bank guarantee, rent-guarantee insurance, or the maximum lawful deposit + advance

Selling later — capital gains (brief)

Residents and non-residents alike: only 50% of the gain is taxable, added to income at progressive rates (effective rate on the full gain typically 0–24%). Deductible: the original IMT / stamp / notary, documented improvement works from the last 12 years, and sale costs / agent fees; the purchase price is inflation-indexed if held > 24 months. Reinvesting the proceeds in another primary residence in Portugal / EU / EEA within 36 months can exempt the gain in full or part.
